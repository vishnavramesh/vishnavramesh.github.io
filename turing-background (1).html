The background

Gray–Scott reaction–diffusion: two chemicals, u and v. u is fed in at rate f, v is removed at rate k, and v catalyses its own production by consuming u. Because u diffuses twice as fast as v, the activator is trapped locally while the inhibitor spreads. That asymmetry is the whole of Turing's 1952 argument, and it is sufficient to produce the patterns you see.

Move the pointer to seed v. Each press cycles to the next palette colour, and a long stroke cycles on its own every couple of seconds. Colour is stored as a third field that diffuses with the pattern, so a brick seed grows a brick colony and mixes only at the borders with a neighbour.

Two things it respects. The loop pauses when the tab is hidden, and under prefers-reduced-motion it computes a few hundred steps once and renders a still. Parameters here are f = 0.0545, k = 0.062, the coral regime.
