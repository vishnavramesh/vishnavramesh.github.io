The problem

Anti-stroke drug candidates bind polymer-like coagulants (such as polyphosphate) along a lattice, where each bound protein occludes neighboring sites. Testing every candidate's binding behavior at the bench costs the lab more than ten hours apiece.

What I built

  • A McGhee–von Hippel probabilistic lattice-binding model in Python (NumPy/SciPy) simulating protein–polymer binding, including cooperative and overlapping-site effects.
  • Extensions covering mRNA- and DNA-based protein binding under the same framework.
  • Simulation of 10+ new polymer-like coagulants, letting the lab screen binding-optimization candidates computationally before committing bench time.

Results

The lab now filters candidates in simulation first, saving over 10 hours of wet-lab work per candidate and focusing ITC experiments on the most promising binders.
